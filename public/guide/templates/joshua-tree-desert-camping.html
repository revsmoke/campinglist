Joshua Tree Camping

For campers and backpackers visiting Joshua Tree National Park, where you bring every drop of water yourself.

Covers a 2-4 night cool-season trip (October-May) for a small group at a park campground or in a backcountry zone on a Recreation.gov backpacking permit. Assumes no water inside the park except at Black Rock and Cottonwood campgrounds, big day-night temperature swings and little or no cell service. Summer visitors should plan around extreme heat; backpackers can drop the campground-only items.

Check before you go

  • Black Rock, Cottonwood, Indian Cove, Jumbo Rocks, Ryan and Sheep Pass campgrounds are reservation-only up to 6 months ahead; Hidden Valley, White Tank and Belle are first-come and are usually full by Friday afternoon from October to May (NPS, as of 2026).
  • Only Black Rock and Cottonwood campgrounds have water. The park recommends at least 1 gallon per person per day and more when hiking in heat; keep extra water in the vehicle.
  • Backcountry camping requires a $6 Recreation.gov permit (1-12 people, up to 14 nights) in one of 15 zones, at least 1 mile from a trailhead, half a mile from roads and 200 ft from trails. Campfires are illegal in the backcountry.
  • Campground fires are allowed only in the provided rings with wood brought from outside the park; a seasonal campfire ban has run from about June 15 to October 1 in recent years. Check current fire restrictions.
  • Temperatures top 85 F from April to September; hike before 10 am or after 4 pm in heat. Flash floods can occur even on sunny days during summer monsoon season.
  • Cell service is extremely limited; download offline maps and the NPS app and share your itinerary. Search and rescue can take hours or days to reach you.
  • Campsite limits: 6 people, 3 tents and usually 2 vehicles; generators only 7-9 am, noon-2 pm and 5-7 pm; first-come sites must be paid within an hour.

Permits & Planning 4/8

  • Campground reservation on Recreation.gov (up to 6 months ahead) Or a first-come plan for Hidden Valley, White Tank or Belle with an early arrival.
  • Backcountry permit if backpacking ($6, 1-12 people, up to 14 nights) Book on Recreation.gov up to 6 months ahead; choose one of 15 zones (as of 2026).
  • Park entrance pass
  • Check current fire restrictions and the heat or storm forecast
  • Itinerary shared with someone at home (no cell service)
  • Offline maps and the NPS app downloaded before arrival
  • Firewood bought outside the park (no gathering)
  • Cash or card for first-come site fees

Water 4/5

  • At least 1 gallon of water per person per day; 2 gallons in heatOnly Black Rock and Cottonwood campgrounds have water.
  • Large camp water jugs (5-7 gallons)
  • Extra water left in the vehicle for emergencies
  • Bottles or bladder for 3 liters on hikes
  • Electrolyte mix

Shelter & Sleep 3/5

  • Tent with sturdy stakes for wind and rocky ground
  • Sleeping bag warm enough for near-freezing nightsFall, winter and spring nights get cold even after hot days.
  • Sleeping pad
  • Shade canopy or tarp
  • Rock-proof stakes and mallet

Clothing 3/6

  • Sun hat and sunglasses
  • Long-sleeve sun shirt and pants
  • Warm layers and beanie for cold nights
  • Sturdy closed-toe shoes
  • Windbreaker
  • Gloves for scrambling and cholla

Kitchen & Food 3/6

  • Stove and fuelFire bans are common; a stove is the reliable option.
  • Cooler and ice
  • Cook kit and utensils
  • Food in sealed containers or the vehicle (ravens, rodents, coyotes)
  • Trash bags
  • Camp chairs and table

Navigation & Safety 5/9

  • Paper map of the park
  • Compass or GPS
  • First aid kit
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • Sunscreen SPF 30+ and lip balm
  • Whistle and signal mirror
  • Comb and tweezers for cholla spines
  • Satellite messenger
  • Vehicle with full tank, good spare tire and extra water

Leave No Trace / Pack-out 3/6

  • Backcountry: WAG bags, or bury waste 200 ft from water (no water nearby anyway)
  • Pack out all trash
  • Pack out toilet paper
  • No fires in the backcountry; campground rings only
  • Stay on trails; do not climb or damage Joshua trees
  • Respect generator hours and quiet hours
