A.T. Section Hike

For hikers doing a 4-10 day section of the Appalachian Trail using shelters, designated campsites and trail-town resupplies.

Assumes a spring-to-fall section hike of roughly 50-150 miles for one to four people, sleeping in or near A.T. shelters and resupplying in towns every 3-5 days. Covers the Appalachian Trail Conservancy's bear-canister recommendation and required-storage zones, the three permit areas (Smokies, Shenandoah, Baxter), regional camping and fire rules and wet-weather gear. Adapt insulation for early spring or late fall and add the permits specific to your section.

Check before you go

  • Permits are required in Great Smoky Mountains NP ($8 per person per night plus $6, or the $44.50 thru-hiker permit, as of April 30, 2026), Shenandoah NP ($9 per person plus $6 on Recreation.gov up to 90 days ahead) and Baxter State Park (free long-distance hiker permit in person); AMC campsites in the Whites cost about $10 per night (ATC, as of 2026).
  • The ATC recommends a hard-sided bear canister for every overnight trip. Canisters are required March 1-June 1 between Jarrard Gap and Neel Gap in Georgia; the Smokies require hanging on provided cables; Shenandoah requires a canister, a provided box or pole, or a 12 ft hang. Bears have defeated hangs and shredded Ursacks in NC and TN.
  • Camping is limited to shelters and designated sites in the Smokies, New Jersey, New York and Connecticut; Connecticut and New Jersey ban campfires; Maine allows fires only at designated sites and no camping above treeline. Check the ATC camping-regulations page for your section.
  • Never sleep with food; cook and eat 200 ft from your tent and keep canisters out of shelters and about 70 steps from where you sleep.
  • Expect rain, mud, slick roots and rock, ticks (Lyme disease) and norovirus in shelters; treat all water and sanitize hands before eating.
  • Trail towns are typically 3-5 days apart; hostel, shuttle and post office hours change with the season, so confirm before you go.

Permits & Planning 6/7

  • Permits for park sections you cross: Smokies, Shenandoah or Baxter Only needed if your section enters these parks; AMC/GMC site fees apply in NH and VT.
  • Section plan with shelters, campsites, daily mileage and bailout roads
  • Resupply plan: towns, mail drops and hostel or shuttle bookings
  • Trailhead transport and overnight parking arrangedSome trailheads require parking permits or have car break-in warnings.
  • Guidebook or app with offline maps and water sources
  • Check ATC trail updates for closures and bear-activity camping bans
  • Emergency contact plan with expected check-ins

Food Storage & Bear Safety 3/5

  • Hard-sided bear canister (ATC recommended; required in some zones)Required Mar 1-Jun 1 Jarrard Gap to Neel Gap (GA); Shenandoah accepts canisters or a 12 ft hang.
  • Hang kit (50 ft cord, carabiner) for the PCT method where hanging is allowedPlan at least 45 minutes to hang properly; bears have learned to defeat hangs.
  • All scented items (toothpaste, sunscreen, cookware) stored with food at night
  • Food to the next resupply plus one extra day
  • Cook and eat 200 ft from your tent; canister about 70 steps away

Shelter & Sleep 3/5

  • Tent, tarp or hammock (shelters fill; some areas require designated sites)
  • Sleeping bag or quilt for the season
  • Sleeping pad
  • Earplugs
  • Bug net and underquilt if hammocking

Clothing 4/8

  • Rain jacket
  • Rain pants or rain kilt
  • Insulating layer
  • Wool or synthetic socks (3 pairs) plus dry camp socks
  • Trail runners or boots
  • Warm hat and gloves
  • Town and sleep clothes
  • Gaiters

Water & Kitchen 4/5

  • Water filter plus backup tablets
  • Capacity for 2-3 liters (dry ridges and late-summer springs)
  • Stove and fuel
  • Pot and spoon
  • Lighter

Navigation & Safety 5/10

  • Map or app with a paper backup
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • First aid kit with blister care
  • Tick removal tool and permethrin-treated clothing
  • Hand sanitizer (norovirus spreads through shelters)
  • Satellite messenger
  • Trekking poles
  • Whistle
  • Battery bank
  • Pack liner or dry bags

Resupply & Town 1/4

  • ID, cash and card
  • Mail-drop boxes labeled with name and ETA
  • Phone and charger
  • Laundry and shower kit

Leave No Trace / Pack-out 3/6

  • Trowel for a 6-8 in cathole 200 ft from water
  • Pack out toilet paper and hygiene products
  • Trash bag for all packaging
  • Use privies where provided
  • Fires only where allowed and in existing rings (none in CT or NJ)
  • Leave shelters clean; never store food inside
