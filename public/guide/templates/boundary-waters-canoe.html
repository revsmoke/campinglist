Boundary Waters Canoe Trip

For paddlers taking a multi-day canoe-and-portage trip in the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness during the May-September quota season.

Assumes a 3-6 night paddle-and-portage trip for a group of up to 9 people and 4 canoes entering on a quota permit from Superior National Forest. Covers permit pickup and the Leave No Trace video, the no-cans/no-bottles rule, designated campsites with fire grates and latrines, bear-resistant food storage and portage-friendly packing. Adapt for shoulder seasons (self-issued permits, cold water) and for current fire restrictions and wildfire closures.

Check before you go

  • Overnight paddle permits are quota-limited May 1-Sept 30 (2026 reservations opened Jan 28 on Recreation.gov); fees were $16 per adult and $8 per youth per trip plus a $6 reservation fee in 2026, and the Forest Service has proposed higher fees from 2027.
  • Permits are valid only for the listed entry point and date; pick up on the entry date or the day before with photo ID, and your whole group must watch the BWCAW Leave No Trace video series.
  • Maximum 9 people and 4 watercraft together anywhere; one campsite per group at Forest Service designated sites with steel fire grates and latrines; 14-day limit per site.
  • Cans and glass bottles are illegal except for fuel, insect repellent and medicines; repackage food and drinks into reusable containers.
  • Bear-aware food storage order (April 1-Nov 30): hang food and scented items at least 12 ft up and 6 ft from the trunk, or use an IGBC-certified bear-resistant container (Superior NF rules, as of 2026).
  • Fires only in the steel grates using small dead-and-down wood; drown and stir before leaving camp or sleeping. Fire bans and wildfire closures happen; check current forest alerts.
  • Pack it in, pack it out: latrines are not trash cans; fish remains, bath and dish water go 200 ft from shore and camp; no out-of-state firewood; drones and fireworks are prohibited.
  • A Minnesota fishing license is required to fish; dump bait water before each portage and pack out unused live bait.

Permits & Planning 5/8

  • BWCAW overnight paddle quota permit for your entry point and date Reserve on Recreation.gov; valid only for the listed entry point and date (quota season May 1-Sept 30).
  • Photo ID for the permit holder or alternate at pickupPickup is allowed on the entry date or one day prior at the listed issue station.
  • Whole group watches the Leave No Trace video seriesPart 3 is shown at permit issue; a seasonal LNT card speeds later trips.
  • Minnesota fishing license if anyone will fish
  • Route plan with campsites and portages shared with a contact
  • Outfitter canoe rental and shuttle booking
  • Check current fire restrictions and wildfire or entry-point closures
  • Campground or outfitter bunk for the night before entry

Canoe & Portage Gear 6/9

  • Canoes (no more than 4 watercraft per group)
  • Life jacket (PFD) for every person, worn on the water
  • Paddles plus one spare per canoe
  • Portage yoke and pads
  • Canoe packs with waterproof liners
  • Dry bags for sleeping gear and electronics
  • Bailer, sponge and painter lines
  • Waterproof map case with BWCAW maps
  • Leave portage wheels home unless using one of the five permitted portages

Food & Bear Storage 6/7

  • Food repackaged: no cans or glass bottlesFuel, insect repellent and medicines may stay in original containers.
  • IGBC-certified bear container or hang kit (50 ft rope, carabiner)Food storage order in effect April 1-Nov 30: 12 ft up and 6 ft out, or certified container.
  • Reusable water bottles and a camp water container
  • Water filter or treatment
  • Stove and fuelFire bans are common in dry spells; do not rely on the grate.
  • Cook kit and utensils
  • Meals for each day plus one extra

Shelter & Sleep 3/5

  • Tent with footprintPitch on the vegetation-free tent pads at designated sites.
  • Sleeping bag
  • Sleeping pad
  • Tarp and cord for a rain kitchen
  • Bug shelter or head nets

Clothing 4/7

  • Quick-dry layers and wool socks
  • Rain jacket and rain pants
  • Warm layer (fleece or puffy)
  • Water shoes or portage boots that can get wet
  • Dry camp shoes
  • Sun hat and sunglasses with retainer
  • Bug jacket or long sleeves for mosquito season

Safety & Navigation 5/10

  • Map and compass
  • First aid kit
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • Whistle clipped to each PFD
  • Repair kit: duct tape, canoe patch, cord
  • Satellite messengerNo cell service in most of the wilderness.
  • Sunscreen and insect repellent
  • EPA-registered bear spray
  • Folding saw for dead-and-down wood only
  • Lighter and matches in a waterproof case

Fishing (optional) 0/4

  • Rod, reel and tackle in reusable containers
  • Live bait in a bucket you can drain before each portage
  • Fillet knife and a plan to dispose of remains 200 ft from shore
  • Current Minnesota regulations for the lakes you will fish

Leave No Trace / Pack-out 4/7

  • Trash bags; pack out everything, including food scrapsBurning trash is illegal.
  • Latrines for human waste only; pack out wipes and hygiene products
  • Bathe and wash dishes at least 200 ft from water
  • Fires only in the steel grate; drown and stir until cold
  • Final camp sweep; no new fire rings, trenches or cut vegetation
  • Clean, drain and dry gear; no out-of-state firewoodPrevents spread of invasive species and emerald ash borer.
  • Dispose of fish remains 200 ft from shore, camps and trails
