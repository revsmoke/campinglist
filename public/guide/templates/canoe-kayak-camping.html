Canoe / Kayak Camping

For paddlers carrying camp in a boat on multi-day lake, river, or sheltered-coast trips.

A starting point for 2-6 night paddle-in trips by canoe or kayak on flatwater, mild rivers, or protected coastal routes. Assumes portages or long carries are possible, everything must survive a capsize, and cold water is a hazard even in summer. Adapt for whitewater, open coast, or tidal trips (more rescue gear, immersion wear, VHF radio) and for area rules such as group-size limits and container bans.

Check before you go

  • Check current entry permits and quotas; popular wilderness routes such as the Boundary Waters require reserved permits in peak season.
  • Check water temperature, not just air temperature; dress for immersion whenever the water is cold.
  • Check wind and weather daily, plus tide and current tables on coastal routes, and plan bailout landings.
  • Check current container, fire, and campsite rules; some areas ban cans and glass bottles or require designated sites.
  • Check current fishing license rules for the state or province if you plan to fish.
  • Lake and river water must be filtered or boiled; plan treatment capacity for the whole group.

Planning & Permits 4/7

  • Entry or overnight permit for the route (quota permits in peak season) Check current quota dates and reservation rules for the area.
  • Float plan filed with someone: route, campsites, return date, who to call
  • Waterproof map or chart in a map case, plus compass
  • Weather and wind forecast; tide and current tables for coastal trips
  • Fishing license for the state or province Carry it on the water; check current regulations for the area.
  • Shuttle, put-in, and take-out logistics arranged
  • Group-size and watercraft limits checked (e.g., 9 people and 4 boats in the BWCAW)

Boat & Paddling Gear 8/16

  • Canoe or kayak suited to the water, with adequate flotation
  • Paddle per person plus one spare per boat
  • PFD (life jacket) per person, properly fitted and worn
  • Whistle attached to each PFD
  • Throw bag or rescue rope
  • Bilge pump and/or bailer, plus sponge
  • Bow and stern painters and tie-down straps for the car
  • Boat repair kit: duct tape, sealant or epoxy putty, spare parts
  • Spray skirt (kayak)
  • Paddle float and rescue sling for kayak self-rescueEssential for sea kayaking; practice re-entry before the trip.
  • Tow line
  • Portage yoke and portage pack (canoe)
  • Deck compass and strobe or light for low light
  • River knife clipped to the PFD
  • Helmet for whitewater
  • VHF radio or satellite messenger for coastal or remote trips

Dry Storage 3/5

  • Dry bags: several small ones labeled by category
  • Food barrel or bear-resistant container with harnessBear-resistant storage is required in some areas; check current rules.
  • Trash bags to pack out everything
  • Waterproof phone case
  • Pack liners for sleeping bag and clothes

Shelter & Sleep 3/6

  • Tent with fly and stakes, plus cord for rocky sites
  • Sleeping bag rated for the expected lows
  • Sleeping pad
  • Tarp with rope for a rain kitchen
  • Groundsheet
  • Pillow

Kitchen & Water 7/10

  • Camp stove and fuel (fires may be restricted)
  • Lighter plus waterproof matches
  • Pots, pan, and cooking utensils
  • Plates or bowls, cups, and cutlery
  • Water filter or purifier plus backup tablets
  • Water bottles or bladder; extra capacity on salt water
  • Food, repackaged out of cans and glass where banned
  • Biodegradable soap and scrubber (wash 200 ft from water)
  • Fire gloves and small saw or hatchet where fires are allowed
  • Soft cooler for the first day or two

Clothing 8/12

  • Quick-dry paddling clothes (no cotton)
  • Wetsuit or drysuit when the water is coldDress for the water temperature, not the air; cold water can incapacitate in minutes.
  • Rain jacket and rain pants
  • Warm layer (fleece or puffy) kept in a dry bag
  • Dry camp clothes and sleep layers
  • Water shoes or sandals with heel straps for wet landings
  • Sun hat with brim, plus a warm hat
  • Sunglasses with a retainer strap
  • Camp shoes
  • Paddling gloves
  • Bug shirt or head net
  • Swimsuit

Safety & First Aid 5/8

  • First-aid kit in a dry bag, with blister and burn care and medications
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • Sunscreen and SPF lip balm
  • Emergency blanket or bivy
  • Knife or multi-tool
  • Insect repellent
  • Signal mirror and flares for coastal trips
  • Spare glasses or contacts

Hygiene & Waste 3/6

  • Trowel for catholes, or use provided latrines; stay 200 ft from water
  • Toilet paper in a zip bag
  • Hand sanitizer
  • Toothbrush and toothpaste
  • Quick-dry towel
  • WAG bags or portable toilet where requiredMany river corridors require packing out human waste; check current rules.

Extras 0/4

  • Fishing gear
  • Lightweight camp chair
  • Camera in a waterproof case
  • Cards or a book
