John Muir Trail Thru-Hike

For hikers thru-hiking the 211-mile John Muir Trail between Yosemite Valley and Mount Whitney over two to four weeks.

Assumes a July-September thru-hike of about 18-25 days, southbound from Yosemite or northbound from Whitney Portal, with two to four resupplies. Covers the continuous-trip permit honored by Yosemite, Inyo National Forest and Sequoia-Kings Canyon, mandatory bear canisters, Whitney Zone waste rules and high-pass conditions. Adapt the resupply count, snow gear and insulation to your start date and pace.

Check before you go

  • One continuous-trip wilderness permit is honored by every agency on the route and is issued by the agency where you start: Yosemite (Happy Isles 15/day, Lyell Canyon 30/day, with a 45/day Donohue Pass exit quota) or Inyo NF (Whitney Portal lottery Feb 1-Mar 15; as of 2026).
  • Allowed bear canisters are required in all of Yosemite, Donohue Pass to Reds Meadow, Duck Lake to Lake Virginia, Dusy Basin, Pinchot to Forester Pass, Kearsarge, Rock Creek/Cottonwood and the Whitney Zone; counter-balance hangs are legal only where canisters are not mandatory.
  • Whitney Zone: pack out solid human waste in the waste kit issued with your permit between Guitar Lake and Whitney Portal; Inyo charges $15/person for Whitney Zone permits plus a $6 fee (as of 2026).
  • Campfires are banned above 9,600 ft in Yosemite, 10,000 ft in the Ansel Adams Wilderness and SEKI, and 10,000-10,400 ft in the John Muir Wilderness, plus many lake basins; check current restrictions.
  • Resupply: General Delivery post offices in Yosemite Valley (year-round) and Tuolumne Meadows (mid-June to mid-Sept); commercial caches at Reds Meadow, Vermilion Valley Resort and Muir Trail Ranch need advance booking and fees (verify current terms).
  • Camping limits include 2-night maximums at Dusy Basin, Charlotte and Kearsarge Lakes, Woods Creek to Vidette Meadow (1 night per lake between Dollar Lake and Glen Pass) and Crabtree/Guitar Lake; no camping at Timberline Lake, Shadow Lake or within 1/4 mile of Thousand Island and Garnet Lake outlets.
  • Expect afternoon thunderstorms, altitude (Whitney is 14,505 ft), early-season snow and swift stream crossings, and mosquitoes through July; no surface water above about 12,400 ft on the Whitney Trail.
  • Inyo quota season is May 1-Nov 1; 60% of its permits release 6 months ahead and 40% two weeks ahead on Recreation.gov (as of 2026).

Permits & Logistics 5/8

  • JMT wilderness permit from the agency where your hike starts Yosemite lottery for Happy Isles/Lyell Canyon with Donohue exit; Inyo lottery for Whitney Portal starts (as of 2026).
  • Whitney Trail Crest exit or Whitney Portal start reserved on your permit Trail Crest exit quota is 25/day; Whitney Portal starts use the Feb 1-Mar 15 lottery (Inyo NF, 2026).
  • Printed, signed permit and Whitney waste kit picked up at the permit station
  • Resupply plan: boxes sent to Tuolumne PO, Reds Meadow, VVR or Muir Trail RanchConfirm current deadlines, fees and pickup hours with each location.
  • Transport plan: YARTS/ESTA buses, shuttles or car shuttle between trailheads
  • Itinerary with daily mileage, camping limits and bailout trailheads shared with a contact
  • Acclimatization night at a mid-elevation campground before starting Whitney Portal, Onion Valley and Horseshoe Meadows campgrounds sit at 8,000-10,000 ft.
  • Travel or rescue insurance

Food Storage & Resupply 3/5

  • Allowed bear canister sized for your longest carryOnly models on the Yosemite/SEKI allowed list; store 50 ft from your sleeping area with the lid closed.
  • Food for the longest leg plus one extra day, repackaged
  • Resupply buckets or boxes sealed, labeled with name and ETA
  • Cash or card for resupply pickup fees and store purchases
  • Fuel allocation per leg and a spare lighter

Shelter & Sleep 3/4

  • Lightweight tent or tarp with bug netting
  • Sleeping bag or quilt rated to 15-25 F
  • Insulated pad (R-value 3 or higher)
  • Pillow and earplugs

Clothing 6/10

  • Trail runners or boots, broken in
  • Wool or synthetic socks (3 pairs)
  • Rain shell plus rain or wind pants
  • Puffy jacket
  • Fleece or mid-layer
  • Sun hoodie and hiking shorts or pants
  • Warm hat and liner gloves
  • Sun hat and sunglasses (dark lenses for snow)
  • Camp or stream-crossing shoes
  • Town clothes in a resupply box

Water & Kitchen 3/6

  • Water filter plus backup chemical treatmentSurface water can carry pathogens; filter, boil or treat everything.
  • Capacity for 2-3 liters, 3 liters for Whitney summit dayNo water above about 12,400 ft on the Whitney Trail after snowmelt.
  • Stove, fuel and lighter
  • Pot, spoon and mug
  • Electrolyte mix
  • Cook and wash 100 ft from water; strain and scatter dishwater

Navigation & Safety 5/13

  • JMT map set or app with offline maps
  • Compass or GPS device
  • Satellite messengerStrongly recommended; report overdue hikers to the Inyo County Sheriff (760-878-0383).
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • First aid kit with blister care and altitude medication
  • Sunscreen and SPF lip balmReapply often; UV is intense above 10,000 ft.
  • Insect repellent and head net
  • Trekking poles
  • Microspikes or ice axe for early-season passes
  • Repair kit: tape, needle and thread, pad patches
  • Knife, whistle and signal mirror
  • Battery bank and cables (recharge at resupplies)
  • Pack liner or dry bags

Whitney Zone & High Passes 3/4

  • Human waste kits: 1-2 per person per day in the Whitney ZonePack out used kits and drop them in the bins at Whitney Portal.
  • Summit-day plan: start early and turn around when storms build
  • Extra layers for wind and cold at 14,505 ft
  • Acclimatization schedule and knowledge of altitude sickness signs

Leave No Trace / Pack-out 4/6

  • Trowel; bury waste 6-8 in deep, 100 ft from water, trails and campsExcept in the Whitney Zone, where everything is packed out.
  • Pack out all toilet paper and hygiene products
  • Trash bag for all packaging; nothing burned or buried
  • Camp on durable surfaces 100 ft from water and obey posted camping limits
  • Fires only where allowed, in existing rings, drowned cold; prefer no fire
  • Hand sanitizer; urinate 100 ft from water and camps
