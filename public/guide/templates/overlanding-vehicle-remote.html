Overlanding / Remote Vehicle Camping

For 4x4 travelers camping self-sufficiently for days at a time far from pavement and services.

A starting point for multi-day vehicle-based trips on remote dirt roads where you carry all of your fuel, water, and recovery gear and may not see another vehicle. Assumes a capable 4x4 with rated recovery points, 1-4 people, and camping from the vehicle in a ground tent, rooftop tent, or trailer. Adapt water and fuel to distance and climate, and match recovery gear to your vehicle's weight and whether you travel solo.

Check before you go

  • Check current road and trail status on the Motor Vehicle Use Map and with the local ranger district or BLM field office; stay on designated routes.
  • Check current fire restrictions; many areas ban campfires seasonally and some states require a campfire permit even for stoves.
  • Check current dispersed-camping stay limits and distance-from-water rules for the land you are on.
  • Carry water and fuel for the whole loop plus a reserve; services can be 100 miles or more apart.
  • Cell coverage is unreliable; carry a satellite communicator and leave your route and return date with someone.
  • Check seasonal hazards: flash floods in washes, snow at elevation, and extreme heat in deserts.

Planning, Permits & Navigation 5/9

  • Route planned on the MVUM or BLM travel map; stay on designated routes
  • Paper maps of the area plus a compass
  • GPS or offline mapping app with route and campsites loaded
  • Trip plan left with someone: route, vehicle, plate, and return date
  • Pre-trip vehicle inspection: fluids, belts, hoses, battery, tires, brakes
  • Campfire permit where your state requires one (e.g., California) Free online in California; required outside developed sites, including for stoves.
  • Land-use permits or passes (parks, OHV areas, tribal lands)
  • Daily five-minute vehicle walk-around
  • Drone (only where allowed) Prohibited in national parks and Wilderness without a permit; check rules for each land unit.

Recovery & Tires 10/18

  • Full-size spare tire (consider a second when traveling solo)
  • Tire repair kit (plugs and patches) and the skill to use it
  • 12V air compressor and tire pressure gaugeAiring down to 20 psi or less helps in sand; air back up before pavement.
  • Traction boards
  • Shovel (full-size or D-handle)
  • Rated recovery points, front and rear, verified before the trip
  • Jack rated for the loaded vehicle, plus a base plate for soft ground
  • Jump starter or jumper cables
  • Ratchet or cam straps to secure all cargo
  • Automotive fire extinguisher, mounted within reach
  • Tire deflator
  • Kinetic recovery rope or snatch strapFor assisted recovery; needs a second vehicle and rated recovery points on both.
  • Soft shackles and rated bow shackles
  • Tree-trunk protector strap and winch extension
  • Snatch block or recovery ring
  • Winch (useful in treeless terrain or when solo)
  • Hitch-receiver shackle mount
  • Recovery gloves

Tools & Spares 3/5

  • Basic tool kit sized to your vehicle: sockets, wrenches, pliers, drivers
  • Duct tape, zip ties, bailing wire, and epoxy
  • Headlamp and work light
  • Spare fluids: engine oil, coolant, brake fluid
  • Spares for known failure points: belts, fuses, relays, hoses

Fuel, Water & Power 3/8

  • Fuel for the whole route plus a reserve; cans secured outside the cabin
  • Water: at least 1 gallon per person per day, more in heat, plus a vehicle reserve
  • Water containers secured against rollover; refill plan
  • Water filter or purifier for backcountry sources
  • Dual battery or portable power station
  • Solar panel
  • 12V fridge or cooler with an ice plan
  • Chargers and cables for devices

Communication 1/4

  • Satellite messenger or PLBRegister a PLB with NOAA (free). Have more than one way to call for help.
  • GMRS, FRS, or ham radio for convoy travel GMRS and ham use require FCC licenses; FRS does not.
  • Cell phone with offline maps and a battery bank
  • Emergency contact card and nearest medical facility noted

Shelter & Sleep 3/6

  • Ground tent, rooftop tent, or in-vehicle sleeping setup
  • Sleeping bag per person rated for the lows
  • Sleeping pad or mattress
  • Awning or shade shelter
  • Camp chairs and table
  • Pillows and blankets

Kitchen & Food 6/8

  • Stove and fuel
  • Lighter and matches
  • Cookware, utensils, plates, and cups
  • Food for all days plus two extra days
  • Food in sealed bins; bear-resistant storage where required
  • Trash bags; pack out all trash
  • Wash bins, biodegradable soap, and towels
  • Dutch oven or grill for fire cooking where fires are allowed

Clothing & Personal 3/6

  • Layers for the full temperature range, including a rain shell
  • Sturdy boots plus camp shoes
  • Sun hat, sunglasses, and sunscreen
  • Work gloves
  • Warm hat and gloves
  • Swimwear and towel

Safety, First Aid & Hygiene 4/7

  • Comprehensive first-aid kit with trauma supplies, plus training to use it
  • Bug-out bag: water and filter, warm layer, shelter, comms, navigationGrab-and-go kit in case you must leave the vehicle.
  • Portable toilet or WAG bags, plus a trowel where catholes are allowed
  • Toilet paper and hand sanitizer
  • Insect repellent
  • Shower bag or wipes
  • Personal medications and spare glasses
