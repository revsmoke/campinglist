Yosemite Wilderness Backpacking

For backpackers taking a multi-night wilderness trip in Yosemite National Park on a trailhead-quota permit.

  • SeasonLate May-Oct (peak July-Sept)
  • Length2-5 nights

Covers a 2-5 night summer backpacking trip from a Yosemite trailhead such as Happy Isles, Lyell Canyon or Cathedral Lakes for a small group. Assumes July-September conditions with snow mostly gone, afternoon thunderstorms possible and bear canister rules in force. Adapt insulation and traction for early-season snow, and add Half Dome gear only if your permit includes the cables.

Check before you go

  • Wilderness permits are quota-limited: 60% are reserved by lottery 24 weeks ahead and 40% release 7 days ahead on Recreation.gov; walk-up permits are scarce late April to mid-October (NPS, as of 2026).
  • Approved bear-resistant canisters are mandatory throughout the Yosemite wilderness and hanging food is illegal; only models on the park's allowed list count (NPS list current as of July 2026).
  • Camp at least 100 ft from water and trails, 4 trail miles from Yosemite Valley, Tuolumne Meadows or Glacier Point, and 1 mile from any road; group limit 15 on trail, 8 off-trail.
  • Campfires are allowed only in existing rings below 9,600 ft and are banned in listed basins and on Half Dome; check current fire restrictions before you go.
  • Pack out all toilet paper; bury human waste 4-6 in deep at least 100 ft from water, trails and camp.
  • Half Dome cables require a separate permit even for backpackers (request it with your wilderness permit); nearly all fatal cable falls happened on wet rock.
  • Snow typically covers high trails November through May; expect afternoon thunderstorms and mosquitoes in early summer. Check current trail conditions and the 14-night May 1-Sept 15 stay limit.

Permits & Planning 6/9

  • Yosemite wilderness permit for your trailhead and entry date Lottery opens 24 weeks ahead; 40% of each quota releases 7 days ahead on Recreation.gov (as of 2026).
  • Half Dome permit if your route includes the cables Needed even for backpackers when the cables are up; request it with the wilderness permit.
  • Photo ID and permit confirmation for pickup at a wilderness center
  • Park entry reservation if one is in effect for your dates Yosemite has used peak-period entry reservations in recent years; check the current rules.
  • Itinerary with a legal first-night camp (4 trail miles from developed areas)Share it with an emergency contact along with your exit date.
  • Check current trail, snow and river-crossing conditions
  • Check current fire restrictions and air quality (wildfire smoke)
  • Topographic map and route plan with bailout trailheads
  • Lodging or campsite plan for the night before your start

Food Storage & Bear Safety 4/5

  • Approved bear-resistant canister (about one per 1-2 people)Hanging food is illegal park-wide. Garcia 812 rentals only at the Valley Welcome Center; Ursack is not on the allowed list.
  • All food, trash and scented toiletries fit inside the canisterIncludes sunscreen, bug spray, lip balm, medications and feminine products.
  • Store the canister 25-50 ft from camp on level ground, away from cliffs and water
  • Food repackaged into bags to save canister space
  • Bear-aware routine: cook away from the tent and never leave food unattendedFood must be stored unless within arm's reach of an awake person.

Shelter & Sleep 3/5

  • Tent with rainfly and footprint
  • Sleeping bag rated to about 20-30 F
  • Insulated sleeping pad
  • Extra guylines and stakes for granite slabs and wind
  • Pillow and earplugs

Clothing 5/9

  • Broken-in boots or trail shoes
  • Wool or synthetic socks (3 pairs)
  • Rain jacketAfternoon thunderstorms are common in summer.
  • Insulating layer (fleece or puffy)
  • Base layers, hiking pants or shorts, sun shirt
  • Warm hat and gloves
  • Sun hat and sunglasses
  • Camp shoes or sandals for stream crossings
  • Grippy gloves for the Half Dome cables (pack them out)Discarded gloves pile up at the base of the cables; carry yours out.

Water & Kitchen 4/7

  • Water filter or purification treatmentTreat all backcountry water.
  • Bottles or bladder for 2-3 liters
  • Stove and enough fuel for the tripWood-burning stoves are not allowed where campfires are prohibited.
  • Pot, spoon and mug
  • Meals for every day plus one extra day
  • Snacks and electrolyte mix
  • Small bottle of biodegradable soap, used 100 ft from water

Navigation & Safety 4/10

  • Map and compass, or GPS app with offline maps
  • Headlamp with spare batteries
  • First aid kit with blister care
  • Satellite messenger or PLBNo reliable cell service in the wilderness.
  • Sunscreen SPF 30+ and lip balm (stored in the canister at night)
  • Insect repellent and head net (early summer)
  • Whistle, knife and repair tape
  • Trekking poles
  • Microspikes or ice axe for early-season passes
  • Thunderstorm plan: summit early and avoid exposed rock when storms build

Leave No Trace / Pack-out 4/6

  • Trowel for a 4-6 in cathole 100 ft from water, trails and camp
  • Bags to pack out all toilet paper and hygiene productsToilet paper may not be buried or burned in Yosemite.
  • Trash bag; pack out everything you bring in
  • Camp on durable surfaces at least 100 ft from water and trails
  • Fires only in existing rings below 9,600 ft; drown ashes coldPrefer a stove; check current fire restrictions.
  • Leave drones and pets at home (prohibited in the wilderness)
